Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds (ソニックレーシング クロスワールド, Sonikku Reeshingu Kurosu Waarudo?) is a kart racing video game serving as a spin-off of the Sonic the Hedgehog series. It is developed by Sonic Team, published by Sega, and was released worldwide on 25 September 2025. An animated short promoting the game was released on 29 August 2025, while a one-shot comic based on the game was released on 4 March 2026 by IDW Publishing. The game features characters from the Sonic the Hedgehog universe, with characters from other Sega properties and pop-culture franchises being added periodically as downloadable content.

The game was released for the PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, Xbox Series X and S, Xbox One, Nintendo Switch, and PC. A Nintendo Switch 2 edition was first released digitally on December 4, 2025, and a physical version followed on March 26, 2026 (April 10, 2026 for Japan), along with a paid Upgrade Pack being made available to those who own the Switch version of the game.


## Gameplay


As a racing game, Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds involves players taking control of characters and Machines as they compete in races against others. During these races, players can obtain items from Item Boxes to attack others or give themselves a boost.

There are four different speed classes, each representing the difficulty level of the CPU racers: Normal Speed, High Speed, Sonic Speed, and Super Sonic Speed. Normal Speed is the slowest speed available, while Super Sonic Speed is the fastest. Super Sonic Speed sets the camera to Dynamic regardless of setting, but is unavailable in online settings where Sonic Speed is the default class.

A new feature in the game is the ability to enter portals to other racing maps, also known as CrossWorlds. When approaching the finish line during the first lap, CrossWorld portals appear in the form of Travel Rings. The first racer to cross a marker on the track can choose one of two randomly selected CrossWorlds. The choice is made by taking either the left or right lane; each lane corresponds to a different CrossWorld. The selected map is then entered by driving through the Travel Ring. One of the map options is always visible, while the second is hidden as a "random" option. During the final lap, the race returns to the original course; that is, the course that was used for the first lap. Alterations are present within the final lap, including scenery changes, exclusive routes, and exclusive items within the new routes.

In addition, Transformation Gates return from Sonic & All-Stars Racing Transformed, allowing Machines to transform between land, water, and flight modes. However, unlike Transformed, only the vehicles' wheels transform, not the entire car. All Machines, including Extreme Gear, gain extra propellers and/or turbines when transformed.

Unlike prior Sonic racing games, while each character has their own vehicle, they can now drive other vehicles from any type, as well as mix-and-match various parts from the other race cars within the same category. This applies to CPU racers as well.

Furthermore, the stunt system done with the right stick has been replaced with a revised Air Trick system. The new system can be controlled with either the left or right stick during a jump.

Gadgets also now play an extremely integral part of stat and perk customization, far more than how Mods and Performance Parts merely altered the vehicles' stats. They are slotted onto the player's license, which can be upgraded the more they race as EXP gain/progression akin to the prior Sonic Racing games. The higher ranked the player's license, the more "points" worth of gadgets they are able to slot in, with Gadgets taking up anywhere between one and three slots. However, due to the shape of the Gadget Slots on the license, certain Gadgets are unable to be combined in certain ways. The maximum capacity is of six total Gadget Points.

Start line turbo boosts are no longer attained by merely tap the accelerate input alongside the countdown. Instead, they are attained by holding down the accelerate button to reach a certain threshold on a circular meter styled akin to an RPM (rotations per minute) meter. Keeping the pointer's indicator within the center of the sweet spot indicated on the gauge (colored bright green) provides the best maximum start line boost, while weaker boosts are triggered within the magenta and darker green zones of the meter, as well as a failure boost happening if the rev line is exceeded (holding down the accelerate button too far past the sweet spot). Failure boosts jam the machine in place for a bit before the driver is able to recover to accelerate normally.

## Machines


For the first time in a Sonic racing game, characters are able to ride on both cars and Extreme Gear, collectively referred to as Machines. Additionally, characters are not bound to any specific vehicle. Some Machines in the game are not used as the default vehicle for any particular character. Instead, the player can freely choose the vehicle they want to ride on. Returning vehicles that had unconventional steering devices in Team Sonic Racing, like the Cross Dozer (which had handles) or the Frog Cruiser (which had joysticks) were given steering wheels for the purpose of animation consistency. There are references to 3 cars that go unused in the game. These are the Twinkle Cradle (Handling type), the Western Locomotive (Power type), and the Aero Formula (Speed type). It is unknown if these will be later additions or if they are unused / cut from the game.


## Racetracks


Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds features two types of tracks: main tracks and CrossWorlds. CrossWorlds are accessed through Travel Rings during the second lap of races, with the player in first place being able to choose between a known option or a completely random CrossWorld depending on which lane they're in when selecting the track. The third lap of the main track transforms in some fashion, including a new pathway or a previously-unavailable shortcut. In certain modes, CrossWorlds can be disabled by the player, allowing them to play three laps of the main tracks instead. Additionally, main tracks can be used as CrossWorlds in certain parts of the game, such as online, or after the player has won all seven Grand Prix. The base game will contain 24 main tracks, with six more being added in the Season Pass, and fifteen CrossWorlds, giving a total of 45 courses.

The 24 main tracks in the base game are split between eight Grand Prixes, where the first three races are regular races featuring CrossWorlds, while the last race consists of a medley between the three courses, with players using Travel Rings to switch between courses for each lap. In a Grand Prix, each main track also contains five Red Star Rings that can be collected during the race, some of which can only be accessed by driving off-road or when specific paths open up on the final lap. Collecting all five Red Star Rings will reward the player with 30 Donpa Tickets.

## Other modes


Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds features nine main game modes. Most modes can be played by 1-4 players locally or up to 12 players online. Below are each of the listed game modes:


## Grand Prix


In this offline single-player and multiplayer mode, players participate in a Grand Prix, each of which contain four rounds. The first three rounds race through designated main courses with a randomized CrossWorld. The fourth and final round is a race through the previous three course, connected by Travel Rings (i.e. first course for the first lap, second course for the second lap, and the transformed third course for the final lap). Players are forced to finish a Grand Prix to unlock other modes and features.

When each round is over, players are given points based on the standing when they finished the race. After all four rounds, the player with the most points wins the Grand Prix. During the final round, the points for first place are slightly higher than other rounds. In the case with multiple players sharing first place, no extra rounds will be played, and they all win.

A new rival system pits the player against a rival racer who races faster than the others, including unique interactions with the rival during the race. Before any Grand Prix begins, the player can choose from two pre-selected rival racers, one more difficult than the other. The player can then change the rival's character and level, although they cannot choose the character they are playing as. To win a Grand Prix, the player must beat or tie with their rival after all rounds are finished, even if they do not get 1st place. A 1st place ceremony will also be shown as long as the player placed 1st overall.

If the player is not satisfied with the result when one round ends, they can spend Donpa Tickets to restart that round. Once the player proceeds to the next round, they will be unable to retry previous rounds without restarting the Grand Prix. If the player spends Donpa Tickets at least once during a Grand Prix and wins, a Donpa Ticket icon will appear on that Grand Prix until the player wins that Grand Prix without retrying.

As aforementioned, Red Star Rings can only be collected in this mode, with a total of 5 for each main course; some courses' Red Star Rings can only be accessible on specific spots available exclusively on either the first or final lap. CrossWorld courses do not have any Red Star Rings to collect.


## Race Park


In this offline single-player and multiplayer mode, players compete in six unique race formats with varying objectives and conditions on a course of their choice, or make a Custom Match with customizable rules, including Speed, Course & CrossWorld, CPU difficulty, and items.

In team-based races, a rule will take effect, where players can earn points for their team by performing actions tied to that rule, which also fills a meter that gives all team members temporary invincibility and increased speed; these effects last longer for members in low positions. At the end of the race, the points from finishing placements are added to each team, with a +15 and +10 point bonus for the team(s) that scored the most points on a rule for double teams and triple teams respectively.

This mode features a rival system similar to Grand Prix, although these rivals take the form of AI Racers and participate in groups of 2-4. The Rival Team and level can be changed while selecting a race format, while there is an option to disable Rival Teams. To beat a Rival Team, the player must beat or tie with the Rival Team in team-based matches or beat all members of a Rival Team in individual matches. By beating a Rival Team three times, the player unlock a new Machine or receive Donpa Tickets. Rival Teams will not appear in Custom Match.

## Time Trial


In this offline single-player mode, players compete for the best time on individual courses and aim for the top of the Leaderboard Rankings. Unlike other modes, only Sonic Speed and Super Sonic Speed are available as the speed classes.

Players are given one of four Ranks depending on their finishing time in a Time Trial, with time requirements to each certain Ranks depending on the course. If the player fails to meet the B Rank time, they will receive a C Rank. If the finishing time is the same as the time requirements, the player will get the lower rank instead (e.g, if the finishing time is the same as the S Rank time requirements, the player will get A Rank instead.) In other words, the player must exceed the time requirements.

For every 3 A ranks minimum earned, the player can unlock new music pieces for the Jukebox. The A ranks can be earned across any speed class chosen. Sonic Speed and Super Sonic Speed versions of one course both count as separate A ranks.

## World Match


In this online mode, players compete in races against 11 other players online via cross-platform matchmaking, although unused player slots are replaced with CPUs. Before a race starts, three randomly-selected main courses can be selected, with a random option that picks one of the three chosen courses at random. Players are then sorted with bots and lower-ranked drivers at the front and higher-ranked drivers at the back.

At the end of a race, players will earn points towards their rank, which starts off at E- and can increase up to Legend 1. As the player ranks up, they obtain an exclusive Gadget or Aura, and points received from lower placements are reduced and could result in a point decrease. Point decreases can lead to a player's rank being demoted, but the player can reach that rank again with no additional reward. Until Update 1.3.1, World Match became temporarily unavailable while a Festival was ongoing.

If a player intentionally quits a match or closes the game while in an online race, they will lose Fair Play Points. If they lose enough Fair Play Points, they will temporarily be unable to play online.

## Festival


In this limited-time online mode similar to World Match, players are instead placed into one of three teams, and must work together to earn the most points for the team. As players progress in the mode, they will unlock rewards themed after the Festival.


## Legend Competition


In this limited-time online mode similar to World Match, players who have reached Legend 9 and above can compete in races against 11 other Legend 9 and above players. This mode features an alternate ranking system consisting of Bronze Grade at 1450-1549 points, Silver Grade at 1550-1649 points, Gold Grade at 1650-1749 points, Platinum Grade at 1750-1849 points, and Emerald Grade at 1850+ points. Players start at 1500 points, and can earn points to rank up to reach better grades. The higher the player's grade the points received are reduced and are more likely result in a point decrease. Titles were obtained for players that reached each grade or were ranked among the top players for points during a Legend Competition.

Starting from Round 2, the starting points is determined based on the grade the player had in the previous round, starting at 1500 points if their grade was below Gold, 1550 points if their grade was Gold, and 1650 points if their grade was Platinum or Emerald.

## Friend Match

## Lobby Menu Tabs



## Customize Machines


The Customize Machines tab allows players to personalize their vehicles. Each machine can be outfitted with a variety of parts, including front and rear components, tires, and visual elements such as decals, auras, and horns. Customization not only affects appearance but can also influence vehicle performance, allowing players to tailor their machine to their preferred racing style. Also, each machine part has it's own stats, it can help players make a stat build they want.


## Customize Gadgets


The Customize Gadgets tab lets players equip special items that enhance their racing experience. Gadgets can range from passive stat boosts to unique abilities that activate during races, providing an extra layer of strategy. Each machine has a limited number of gadget slots, so players must carefully choose the right combination to suit their playstyle. Some gadgets may improve acceleration, while others strengthen defenses or increase item effectiveness. The system encourages experimentation and fine-tuning, as certain gadgets may synergize better with specific machines or race conditions. The player starts off with a limited number of Gadgets, and can earn more by finishing races and ranking up in World Matches.


## Character & Machine


The Character and Machine tab allows players to select their racer and assign a machine. Characters come with their own strengths and traits, and when paired with certain machines, they can unlock unique synergies. This tab provides a straightforward way to switch drivers and vehicles without going into deep customization menus. It also acts as a quick preview of your current setup, showing both the chosen character and their equipped machine.


## Friend List


The Friend List tab serves as the social hub of the game, allowing players to connect with others. From here, players can view their friends, check their online status, and send or accept invitations to join lobbies. The feature makes it easier to coordinate multiplayer sessions and stay engaged with a regular group of racers. Whether teaming up for cooperative modes or competing head-to-head, the friend list ensures players can seamlessly race together.


## Extras


There are 70 challenges in the game (with 10 hidden challenges), split into seven categories with ten challenges each. By completing a challenge, the player unlocks a title named after the challenge, and their Chao moves closer to a reward; completing more challenges allows the Chao to reach the reward, which can either be Donpa Tickets or a special title. By completing the first 6 categories, the Secret category will be unlocked which contains 10 more difficult challenges to complete.

## Character themes


Every character has their own ceremony jingle which plays if they get first place in a Grand Prix. Most songs are based on a theme associated with them from past games, aside from Vector, Charmy, Egg Pawn, Sage, Wave, Storm, Tangle, Whisper, and Hatsune Miku, whose jingles are the only ones to not be a character theme of theirs, the team they belong to, or songs otherwise directly associated with the characters (or in Miku's case, the game or media she originated from).

In addition to having a ceremony jingle, downloadable characters have two additional themes that play. The first is on the character select screen when hovering over them, which will then continue playing on the main menu. The second is their own race results background music, playing after the race clear jingle is finished and overriding the standard race results music.


## Cast


In the English dub, this is the first game where Robbie Rist and Fred Tatasciore voice Zazz and Storm, as well as the first game where Storm has voice acting in languages other than English and Japanese.

In the French dub, this is the last game where Benoît Allemane, who passed away 8 months before the game's release, voices Zavok.

In the Italian dub, this is the first game where Luca Airaghi and Davide Garbolino voice Vector and Zazz (the former replacing Matteo Brusamonti from Sonic X Shadow Generations for unknown reasons).

In the German dub, this is the first game where Alexander J. Beck and Wolff von Lindenau voice Zavok and Dodon Pa, following the death of their original voice actor, Klaus Lochthove, in 2023.

In the Spanish dub, this is the first game where Marta Moreno and Domènec Farell voice Wave and Dodon Pa and the final game to feature Alfonso Vallés as Vector due to retiring in 2026.

Version 1.4.1 introduced a new Mandarin Chinese dub in 23 June 2026, making it the first game in the series to include it. It also makes it the first game since Sonic Generations that a new dubbing localization was introduced.

With the exception of Hatsune Miku and Red (with the latter using archival voice in Angry Birds game), all of the downloadable characters in the game lack voice lines, with most of them using sound effects from their own media in place of actual voices, while the announcer is used after races and during victory ceremonies. Steve, Alex, Creeper, Classic Sonic and Amigo are the only characters who are actual silent characters. According to Takashi Iizuka, the reasoning for the non-Sonic characters lacking voice lines is due to Sonic Team wanting to avoid licensing issues with external companies in regards to rival interactions with other characters, as giving them voices would also mean they would need to have interactions.

Jack Fletcher was the voice director for the English dub, Akane Maeda and Keiichiro Miyoshi were the voice directors for the Japanese dub, Antoine Nouel was the voice director for the French dub, Chiara Preziozi was the voice director for the Italian dub, Tim Henneman and Michael Hülsmann were the voice directors for the German dub, and Albert Vilcan was the voice director for the Spanish dub (replacing Guillermo Reinlein).
